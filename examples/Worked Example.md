## 1. What are they actually anxious about?

The posting spends five bullets on **stock accuracy and replenishment** and one on technology. **That ratio
is the tell.** They are not short of systems people; **their shelves are wrong and nobody can say why.**

| Their anxiety | This applicant |
|---|---|
| Stock on the system does not match stock in the store | 🟢 Ran cycle-counting and reconciliation across 14 depots |
| Replenishment fires late and nobody notices until it is empty | 🟢 Built the exception alerting that replaced a daily report |
| Two warehouse systems from an acquisition, neither retired | 🟡 Has migrated one system, never run two in parallel under load |
| Suppliers blame the retailer and vice versa | 🔴 **No evidence. Never held a supplier relationship** |

## 2. What would they be measured on?

| Likely outcome | Can they point at it? |
|---|---|
| Stock accuracy above target within two peak seasons | 🟢 Yes, with a before-and-after |
| One warehouse platform, not two | 🟡 Migrated *to* a platform; never decommissioned a live one |
| Availability holding through Christmas peak | 🟢 Four peaks, none of them quiet |
| Supplier onboarding time down | 🔴 **Not their ground at all** |

## 3. What is rare here?

**Grocery peak experience combined with having built the alerting rather than bought it.** Most applicants
will have one. **Retail supply chain is full of people who have configured a package** — this applicant
has run the operational consequence of getting it wrong at 2am in December.

## 4. Pre-mortem — eighteen months in, it failed. Why?

1. 🔴 **The supplier-facing half.** Two of the four outcomes point outward at trading partners, and their
   entire record points inward at operations. **Most likely failure path.**
2. **Retiring a live warehouse system is a different discipline from launching one**, and the posting
   assumes they are the same.
3. **"Head of" here may sit under a commercial director rather than a technology one**, which changes
   whose priorities win.

🔴 **Read the row, not a total — and there is no total.** A DELIVER of 3 with a whole half of the job
unevidenced is a different proposition from a flat 4-4-4, and **a FIT of 12 built from 5-3-4 is worth
pursuing where a 12 built from 4-4-4 usually is not.**

🟢 **The REQS count is what caught the real problem.** Both outright gaps are supplier-side, which is the
same half of the job DELIVER was uneasy about — **two independent readings landing on one thing is a
finding**, and it is why the first open question below sits ahead of salary.
