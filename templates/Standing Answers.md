# Standing Answers

**Every application asks the same eight things.** Writing them fresh each time produces eight slightly
different versions of the same truth, which is exactly the inconsistency a recruiter notices when they
read the form next to the CV next to the profile.

**So they get written once, here, and reused.** Refine them as they get used; do not rewrite them per
application.

> 🔴 **The narrative answers below are the load-bearing ones.** They appear in the cover letter, in the
> form's free-text boxes, on the first phone call and in the interview. **If those four disagree, the
> application is damaged**, and nobody will tell the applicant why.

---

## 1. Why are you leaving? / Why are you looking?

**The most-asked question in any process, and the easiest to answer badly.**

**TBC — elicit this properly, it is not a one-line answer.**

The version that works is short, forward-facing and true, and it does not criticise the current employer.
An interviewer is listening for one thing: *will this person say the same about us in two years?*

| Situation | The shape that works | The trap |
|---|---|---|
| **Wants more scope** | What they want to do next, and why it is not available where they are | Sounding entitled, or implying they were blocked unfairly |
| **Redundancy or at risk** | Plain statement of fact, no apology, no detail about others | Over-explaining. It is common and it is not a mark against them |
| **Something went wrong** | The shortest true version, then forward | Litigating it. Nobody in an interview is a neutral arbiter |
| **Money** | Real, and fine to say once — but pair it with something about the work | Making it the whole answer, which reads as "will leave for £2k" |

**Whatever is written here is used consistently everywhere.** If the honest reason is one they would
rather not lead with, find the true framing they are comfortable repeating — do not invent a different
reason.

## 2. Why this company? / Why this role?

**Written per application** — but the *method* is standing:

- Something specific from the posting or the company that a competitor could not claim.
- Connected to something the applicant has actually done.
- **Never a compliment.** "I admire your commitment to innovation" is what everyone writes.

**TBC — record here the two or three things that genuinely draw them**, since those recur across
applications and are the raw material each time.

|---|---|
| **Notice** | TBC |
| **Earliest realistic start** | TBC |

**Volunteer a long notice early, not late.** Anything beyond a month can change a hiring plan, and finding
that out at offer stage wastes everyone's time. **State it as a fact, not an apology** — a real notice
period signals a role with genuine handover obligations.

choose the answer that is literally true and address the nuance in the cover letter rather than guessing.

## 7. References

**Not for the CV** — the writing standard keeps them off it. But forms sometimes ask at submission.

| Name | Relationship | Contact held? | Asked permission? | Last spoken to |
|---|---|---|---|---|

**Three rules:**

- 🔴 **Never list someone who has not agreed.** It is the single most common way to annoy a former manager.
- **Ask before the first application, not when an offer is imminent.** People change jobs and email
  addresses.
- **Brief them.** A referee who knows which role and which two things to emphasise is worth three who do
  not. Send them the role page.
- 🔴 **Contact details live outside this wiki.** Per `PRIVACY.md`, other people's contact details are not
  recorded here. Note that a reference exists and has agreed; keep the details in the applicant's own
  contacts.
